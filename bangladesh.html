    Bangladesh

    A country of rivers, delta landscapes, cultural heritage and diverse natural environments.

    🌏 Geography

    Bangladesh sits on the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna delta, the largest river delta in the world, making it low-lying, fertile and crossed by hundreds of rivers and tributaries.

    🎭 Culture

    Bengali culture includes rich traditions of literature, music, food, festivals and crafts, with Pohela Boishakh (Bengali New Year) as one of its most widely celebrated occasions.

    🐅 Wildlife

    The Sundarbans, the world's largest mangrove forest, straddles the coast of Bangladesh and India and is the last major stronghold of the wild Bengal tiger.

    📜 History

    The Bengal region has been home to successive kingdoms and empires for over two thousand years, including the Pala and Mughal periods, and was long a centre of trade in textiles and agriculture.

    Bengal came under British colonial administration in the 18th century. Following the 1947 partition it became East Pakistan, and after the 1971 Liberation War it emerged as the independent nation of Bangladesh.

    🌊 Major Rivers

    Padma

    The main distributary of the Ganges as it flows through Bangladesh, eventually joining the Meghna.

    Jamuna

    The Bangladeshi name for the main channel of the Brahmaputra River after it enters the country.

    Meghna

    Formed by the union of several rivers, it carries combined delta waters to the Bay of Bengal.

    Karnaphuli

    A key river in the southeast, flowing past Chattogram and supporting the country's main seaport.

    🗺️ Divisions

    Dhaka

    The capital division, centred on the densely populated capital city of Dhaka.

    Chattogram

    Home to the Chittagong Hill Tracts, the port city of Chattogram, and the Cox's Bazar coastline.

    Khulna

    Bordering the Sundarbans mangrove forest in the southwest.

    Rajshahi

    A northwestern division known for its agriculture, including mangoes and silk.

    Sylhet

    A northeastern division known for tea gardens and hilly terrain.

    Barishal

    A low-lying southern division shaped by its many rivers and canals.

    Rangpur

    A northern division bordering India, largely agricultural.

    Mymensingh

    A north-central division separated from Dhaka division in 2015.

    📍 Landmarks

    Sundarbans

    A UNESCO World Heritage mangrove forest and tiger habitat.

    Cox's Bazar

    Home to one of the world's longest natural sea beaches.

    Paharpur

    Ruins of a major Buddhist monastery dating to the 8th century.

    Ahsan Manzil

    A 19th-century palace on the Buriganga River in Dhaka.
